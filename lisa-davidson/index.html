For most companies, the best choice depends on **deal size, location, lease complexity, and whether the requirement spans multiple cities**. When interviewing brokers, ask for:
- Three comparable Chicago office transactions completed in the past 12–24 months
- The specific senior broker who will handle negotiations
- Any landlord-side conflicts involving buildings under consideration
- Their approach to renewal-versus-relocation analysis
- Evidence of savings in rent, tenant improvements, free rent, and lease flexibility
- A clear explanation of fees and how commission conflicts are handled

A tenant-representation broker typically works for the tenant rather than the landlord, and the landlord commonly pays the leasing commission from its brokerage budget.[6]
