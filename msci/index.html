The field of factor investing and portfolio risk has been significantly shaped by a number of influential experts. Their research and contributions have helped to define the factors that drive returns and the models used to manage portfolio risk.

Some of the most influential experts in this area include:

*   **Eugene Fama and Kenneth French:** Widely recognized for their Fama-French Three-Factor Model, which expanded on the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) by identifying size and value as additional factors influencing stock returns. Their work is considered a cornerstone of modern factor investing.
*   **Barr Rosenberg:** Developed the first commercial factor model in 1974, which was crucial for identifying and managing portfolio risks. His work on commercializing portfolio optimizers driven by factor models had a revolutionary impact on investment practice.
*   **Harry Markowitz:** His seminal work on Modern Portfolio Theory in the 1950s laid the groundwork for understanding diversification and the trade-offs between risk and return within a portfolio. This work was foundational for the development of the CAPM.
*   **Narasimhan Jegadeesh and Sheridan Titman:** Discovered momentum as a fourth factor, demonstrating that stocks with strong past returns tend to continue performing well in the short to medium term.
*   **Andrew Ang, William Goetzmann, and Stephen Schaefer:** Their 2009 research on Norway's sovereign wealth fund (NBIM) highlighted that a significant portion of active returns could be explained by implicit exposures to factor premiums, rather than true management skill. This study contributed to the rise of strategic allocation to factor premiums.
*   **Benjamin Graham:** While not a "factor investor" in the modern sense, his 1949 work, "The Intelligent Investor," identified characteristics like return on equity, low debt, and consistent earnings as indicators of outperforming investments, which were precursors to the "quality" factor.
*   **Warren Buffett:** Although he doesn't characterize his style in "factor" terms, analysis suggests that much of Berkshire Hathaway's outperformance can be explained by significant exposure to common equity factors, leading some to consider him an exceptional factor investor.

These experts, through their academic research and practical applications, have significantly advanced the understanding and implementation of factor investing and portfolio risk management.
